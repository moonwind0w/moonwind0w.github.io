About me.

Hello.

I’m from Kansas City, born on the Kansas side, but I’ve lived most of my life just across the state line in Missouri. I was born in 2002 and grew up in a suburb outside the city with my parents and my older sister. Childhood in the early 2000s felt like a whirlwind of shifting culture and rapid technological change, and I always felt a strange awareness of how quickly things were moving. I became acquainted with the reality of death at a very young age, and I believe that informed my hyperawareness of the passage and inevitability of time. This sentiment bleeds into my art and my personal work. It was fascinating to grow up alongside the rise of social media platforms, digital communication, and the art of capturing the world around us. It truly is a strange time to be alive. I am almost constantly engaging in personal research and personal projects regarding digital preservation and relevant cultural and historical research for my generation, a generation primarily raised online and amidst the technology revolution. I have taken it upon myself to become somewhat of a “citizen internet archivist.” Through my social media work, I have not only deepened my connection with like-minded individuals but also acquired valuable skills in marketing and outreach, which have helped bring attention to the importance of archival work and historical preservation.

As I learned more about archival work and web preservation, I began to see Flickr as a source of all-encompassing inspiration for my outlook on my personal art. In 2022, I started the “tvwishes” project where I curated collections of overlooked images. I found myself drawn to pages on Flickr that were clearly amateur in nature but poised in emotion. Non-traditional, even accidental, images of daily life taken 20 years ago and left to pick up digital cobwebs. I yearned to dig through these webs, dust them off, and show others the beauty I found in the everyday, the familiarity we all shared, and the comfort that some things never change. These feelings and sentiments inform the art I make. I have always been a collector and a keeper of memories, building personal archives since I was a child, without even knowing. I am not a professional in any sense of the word, but I let that amateurism, the tons of experimental photos I take, serve as my own personal archive of play. My art projects on “moonwind0w” are always healing my inner child in ways I didn't expect. I can now shape the ideas I have had since I was a kid into a format that resonates with like-minded people around the world, and that is simply lovely.

I shoot exclusively with digital cameras, camcorders, and tech from pre-2010. Point-and-shoot digital cameras are wonderful for beginners and, with little technical knowledge, allow you to capture pretty decent images. Beyond the practicality, late 90s and early 2000s digital cameras have so many quirks, from fun body types to different sensors, accessories, interfaces, and more. They are accessible and typically pretty user-friendly. I am an avid estate sale goer, and I'd say 80% of my digicams have come from them. I get asked online a lot about how to find affordable digital cameras, and that's my biggest suggestion. Most of the time when I purchase one from an estate sale, the camera still has photos from the previous owners on it, which is both sad and beautiful. I never have the heart to delete them, so I save them to a hard drive. And while I don’t intend to share them online, it’s cool to have a little lost-and-found photo archive.
